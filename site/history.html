Reguengo, Portalegre · Serra de São Mamede · Alto Alentejo, Portugal

History · 1534 – today

The Highest-Rated and Highest-Ranked Amphora (Talha) Wine in Portugal

Vinha dos Padres is an altitude amphora (talha) wine estate in Reguengo, Portalegre, in the Serra de São Mamede Natural Park, Alto Alentejo, Portugal. Our first vintage, Quinta da Vinha dos Padres 2022 red 3M12M, was ranked #32 in James Suckling's Top 100 Wines of Portugal 2025 with 94 points. No amphora (talha) wine in Portugal scored or ranked higher. Our story begins almost five centuries ago, in Paris in 1534.

1534–1537

Paris to Rome: The First Companions

On 15 August 1534, at Montmartre in Paris, Ignatius of Loyola and six fellow students of the University of Paris made a private vow of poverty and service. They were not yet a religious order, only a group of friends bound by a personal spiritual pact. Among them were the Portuguese nobleman Simão Rodrigues and the Navarrese Francis Xavier.

In November 1536 the companions set out on foot together, across Lorraine, Switzerland and the Tyrol to Venice, where Ignatius was waiting, and reached Rome in November 1537.

The first Jesuits on foot

1537–1540

Rome: The Society of Jesus Is Founded

In 1539 the companions met in a small house near today's Piazza Venezia in Rome and drafted the Formula of the Institute, the founding blueprint of the order. On 27 September 1540 Pope Paul III approved it in the bull Regimini militantis Ecclesiae, formally establishing the Society of Jesus (Companhia de Jesus) as a religious order.

1540

The First Jesuits Arrive in Lisbon

At the invitation of King John III (D. João III), the first Jesuits travelled to Portugal by two routes. Both set out from Rome in March 1540. Simão Rodrigues went by sea and reached Lisbon on 17 April. Francis Xavier rode overland with the ambassador Dom Pedro de Mascarenhas, across northern Italy and France, then through Madrid, Badajoz, Elvas and Évora, crossing the Tagus from Aldeia Galega (Montijo) to Lisbon on 30 June 1540.

Xavier sailed for India in 1541. Rodrigues stayed, and Portugal became the Society's first province in the world in 1546.

1551

Évora: The Jesuit Base in the Alentejo

From Lisbon the Jesuits turned east. In 1551 Cardinal Henrique entrusted them with the College of the Holy Spirit in Évora, raised to a university on 1 November 1559. Évora became the launching point for every Jesuit foundation in the Alentejo.

Jesuit road

1605

Évora to Portalegre: The Jesuit College of São Sebastião

In 1605 the Jesuits founded the College of São Sebastião in Portalegre, reached from Évora through Evoramonte, Estremoz, Fronteira and Monforte, at the foot of the Serra de São Mamede.

1605–1759

The Jesuit Vineyards of Reguengo, Serra de São Mamede

From Portalegre the fathers settled the high valley of Reguengo for its altitude, water and granite and quartzite soils, ideal for agriculture and winemaking. For more than 150 years they farmed and made wine across Reguengo parish to sustain their college.

1759

Expulsion of the Jesuits: Reguengo Passes to the Crown

In 1759 King José I, under the Marquis of Pombal, expelled the Society of Jesus from Portugal. Jesuit lands and vineyards in Reguengo were confiscated by the Crown, and the fathers were shipped into exile to Civitavecchia, then in the Papal States.

Exile by sea

1795

The Jesuit Vineyards of Reguengo Become Vinha dos Padres

The Crown divided the former Jesuit estate. In 1795 João da Fonseca Achioli Coutinho bought the vineyard known as "Quinta dos Padres" ("Farmstead of the Priests"), recorded as Vinha dos Padres, "Vineyard of the Priests", in municipal transaction records that still exist today. The Achioli family helped establish the village of Reguengo and held the vineyard until 1876.

1876–1953

The Centenary Vines Are Planted

The estate changed hands several times. Between about 1876 and 1890 the bush vines that still grow at Vinha dos Padres were planted. In 1931 they were entered in Portugal's new national vine register (Registo Vitícola, 1930), as about 6,000 vines. Today, only about 2,000 of the original centenary bush vines survive.

Vila Maria, the villa crowning the estate, was built in the 1920s, and its cellar was filled with talhas: clay amphorae handmade in Campo Maior between 1905 and the 1930s.

Centenary bush vines

1953–1980s

The Original Quinta da Vinha dos Padres Amphora (Talha) Wine

Regional landowner António Trindade Crespo acquired Vinha dos Padres and made the original Quinta da Vinha dos Padres talha wines, red and white, into the 1980s. Amphora winemaking here is a documented estate tradition, not a modern trend.

1980s–2010

Abandoned, the Old Vines Grow Untouched

After António Trindade Crespo's death the estate was caught in an inheritance dispute. For almost three decades the bush vines grew undisturbed, without intervention.

2010–2020

A Quiet Decade

In 2010 the property was bought by Edward and Jane, a couple from New Zealand. The vines were kept, but no wine was made.

2020

A Winemaking Family Returns

In 2020 the Italo-Portuguese Silveira Catemario di Quadri family acquired Vinha dos Padres, continuing a lineage that runs from Tapada de Coelheiros in Alentejo (1980–2015) back to Duca Catemario di Quadri in Friuli (c. 1900–1964) and Catemario wines (1964–1986).

2022

The Revival of Amphora (Talha) Wine at Altitude

Rua do Saco and Quinta da Casquinha join the estate. The first modern vintage is made by hand at 497–566 metres with consulting enologist Susana Esteban: centenary vines, hand-harvested grapes and fermentation in the historic Campo Maior talhas of Vila Maria, a method brought to the Alentejo more than 2,000 years ago.

Vinha dos Padres is the first amphora (talha) wine producer in Portugal to scientifically and methodically macerate and age the same blends for different periods in amphoras (talhas), to show clearly how the vessels influence the wine over time: hence the code in the top left corner of our labels. Before each harvest the talhas are lined with pez, a resin coating that seals the porous clay, following the traditional pesga process; as the wine rests, a fine layer of flor, a natural veil of yeast and grape solids, rises to the surface and protects it from the air.

2025

Caldeiras Joins, and a Top 100 Debut

The nearby Caldeiras property and its younger vines are acquired and added to the estate. Quinta da Vinha dos Padres 2022 red 3M12M, our first ever harvest, is ranked #32 with 94 points in James Suckling's Top 100 Wines of Portugal 2025, the highest-rated and highest-ranked amphora (talha) wine in the country.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is amphora (talha) wine?

Amphora (talha) wine is made by fermenting grapes in large clay vessels called talhas, a tradition practised in Portugal's Alentejo region for more than 2,000 years. Read our guide to amphora wine →

Which is the best-rated amphora (talha) wine in Portugal?

Quinta da Vinha dos Padres 2022 red 3M12M was the highest-ranked and highest-rated amphora (talha) wine in James Suckling's Top 100 Wines of Portugal 2025 (94 points, #32). James Suckling also rated the 2022 white 3M12M 93 points, and Robert Parker's Wine Advocate awarded 94 points to the 2022 white 3M12M and the 2022 red 3M12M.

Where is Vinha dos Padres?

In Reguengo, Portalegre, inside the Serra de São Mamede Natural Park in Alto Alentejo, Portugal, at 497–566 metres of altitude.

Who owns and makes Vinha dos Padres wines?

Vinha dos Padres is owned by the Silveira Catemario di Quadri family, who acquired the estate in 2020. The wines are made with consulting enologist Susana Esteban.

How old are the Vinha dos Padres vines?

At least 130 years old. They are estimated to have been planted between 1876 and 1890, if not earlier, predate Portugal's 1930 national vine register and were recorded as already old in 1903.
